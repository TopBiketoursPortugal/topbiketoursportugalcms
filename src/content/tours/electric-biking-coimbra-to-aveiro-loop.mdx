  ## **Explore Portugal: Coimbra to Aveiro Cycling Loop – Perfect for Leisure
  Riders**


  ### **Cycle along Portugal’s longest historic railway — 175 km of scenic
  cycling paths and well-marked routes**


  Three river valleys — the Mondego, the Dão and the Vouga — and three quarters
  of the riding on a railway line that has been converted into a cycling path.
  That is the whole reason this tour is graded easy. Railway engineers could not
  build steep gradients, so the climbing on this route averages about 11 metres
  per kilometre: real, but handed to you in long shallow doses rather than
  walls. You finish each day in a city worth arriving early for — Coimbra and
  its university, founded in 1290, Viseu in the Dão wine country, Aveiro on its
  lagoon.


  **The package price includes standard bikes.** Electric bikes are an optional
  upgrade at 510€ per person, or 278€ on the reduced option — see Extras under
  the pricing tables. Plenty of riders take them. On this particular route you
  do not need one to get to Aveiro.


  ### **Aveiro and Coimbra bike tour Loop Route description:**


  * Approximately 75% of the cycling route will follow a former railway trail
  and designated low-traffic cycling roads. The remaining 25% will run along
  paved roads with minimal vehicular traffic.

  * 2,607 m (8,553 ft) of cumulative climbing and 2,609 m (8,560 ft) of
  cumulative descent, spread across five riding days

faqs:
  - question: Are electric bikes included in the price?
    answer: >-
      No. The package price covers a **standard bike**, and that is what most of
      this route is designed around. Electric bikes are an **optional upgrade at
      510€ per person**, or **278€ on the reduced option**, listed under Extras
      with the rest of the supplements. If you want one, say so at booking so we
      can reserve it in your frame size.
  - question: Should I pay for the e-bike upgrade on this particular tour?
    answer: >-
      On most of our tours it is the obvious call. On this one it is genuinely
      optional, because three quarters of the route sits on a converted railway
      line and the climbing averages about **11 m per kilometre**. Riders who
      take the upgrade here usually do it for one of three reasons: a group with
      mixed fitness that wants to stay together, a knee that does not love
      repeated effort, or simply wanting the days to feel shorter. If none of
      those is you, the standard bike is enough.
  - question: What makes this route easy enough for a first cycling trip?
    answer: >-
      The gradient. Railway lines were graded for trains, so the long former-rail
      sections climb very gradually — and the route links **historical Coimbra**
      to the **waterways and salt pans of Aveiro** in daily stages of 36 to 56 km
      (22 to 35 mi), or 36 to 62 km (22 to 39 mi) on the premium itinerary. It is
      one of the few multi-day routes we would put a first-time
      cycling traveller or a family with children on without caveats.
  - question: Do you brief riders on the bike and safety before departure?
    answer: >-
      Yes—before your first ride we set your **saddle height and fit**, run
      through **gearing**, **braking** and **safe path etiquette**, and go over
      the day's route. If you have taken the e-bike upgrade, that briefing also
      covers **assist modes** and **battery management**.
  - question: Is the route mainly on bike paths or roads?
    answer: >-
      It’s a **mixed network**: quiet rural roads, **cycle-friendly avenues**,
      canal-side paths near Aveiro, and sections through towns. We favor
      **low-traffic segments** and clearly mark any urban crossings. About 75%
