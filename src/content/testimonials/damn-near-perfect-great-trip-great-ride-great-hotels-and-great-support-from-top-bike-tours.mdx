  We had friends who went on a Portugal bike tour last year and we found Top
  Bike Tours as an alternative and went with them. They were in between in price
  between 3 vendors.

  They were absolutely sensational. Everything was nearly perfect (there were
  1-2 minor GPS glitches).

  It takes a lot of trust these days to do this. I'd never arranged a tour like
  this before. 10 people, 5 couples, all with different arrivals and needs.

  Communications were quick and articulate, in English. Customizations (we added
  another night, we had 5 couples all coming into Porto on different flights, we
  had one cyclist who wanted a non E bike) were all taken care of accurately.

  We were all met at the airport without a hitch (except for one couple who
  forgot they were being met and stranded the Top Bike Tours employee).

  The orientation was great. maybe could've used a little more instruction on
  the nuances of the GPS (like how to customize the trails, interpret the
  legends, etc) but we figured it out. 2 cyclists got a formal GPS unit; the
  rest of us used the "Ride With GPS" app, which BTW worked great on airplane
  mode to save on phone battery.

  They supplied a rainproof single right sided pannier which was excellent,
  adequate \# of chargers, 2 extension cords, keys to remove batteries (one
  rider's battery would not charge in the bike so they had to remove it); chain
  lube, bells, a rear rack, and repair kit as well as rapid contact via What's
  App (which 2 of us couldn't get to load).

  The last day we apparently couldn't be booked at their go-to Hotel in
  Santiago, so we were booked at the Hotel Compostela (entirely fine, clean and
  modern) but we had to walk our bikes about 4 blocks to another hotel where we
  could drop them off; the receptionists there gave us a hard time so we
  texted/What's app'ed Sergio and he called them and straightened it out.

  The trip included luggage transfer between hotels, transfer from Porto airport
  to Hotel Trindade and then back from Santiago de Compostela to Porto airport
  on the day of our flights.


  First of all, Sergio, Thelmo, and Miquel were all superb. Friendly, excellent
  English, on time, helpful, etc.

  The trip included a walking tour of Porto and a tour of one of the Port Lodges
  across the river, but as we'd been there for several days and had a customized
  tour of the Cockburn/Symington lodge through a personal friend who was a wine
  rep in the USA, we skipped it.

  The stops were terrific. On day one, Miguel led us out of town to the border
  of Porto and we were on our own.

  I will say...the hotels were absolutely first rate. I knew 2 (Paradors) would
  be superb. I was unprepared for just how nice they were. I would also have to
  say that Torre do Rio in Caldas des Rei was on the short list of the most
  gorgeous hotels/grounds I've ever seen. But the more modern hotels were just
  fine, sparkling clean, modern, spacious almost invariably friendly and helpful
  receptionists. Every one had a typical over the top Euro breakfast - pastries,
  scrambled/hard boiled eggs, european bacon, sausages, breads, cold cuts,
  fruits, great coffee, juices, etc.


  In most cases upon arrival they recognized the Top Bike Tours name and they
  immediately directed us to a place where we could chain up and charge the
  bikes; in some cases (Parador in Pontevedra, for example) we had to leave the
  bikes chained up outside in a courtyard and bring in the batteries, which was
  a minimal bother. The hotel restaurants, when we had to rely on them (Hotel
  Suave Mar in Esposende) were good to excellent; in fact at Suave Mar my wife
  and I ate at the bar and the salads and burger were first rate. It was one of
  the ten best caesar salads i have ever had.


  But the unanimous consent of our 10 riders were the hotels were all terrific.


  The bikes (8 Scott Subs; a Trek manual mountain bike, and I forget the 10th
  Ebike) were all perfect. we had one flat, one slow leak; that was it. We rode
  on everything from dedicated bike paths by State highways to cobblestone
  (accursed stuff...) to gravel, crushed stone, boardwalks, sand, forest paths)
  and the bikes did great. They were super comfortable (we had 2 people with
  back issues and they tolerated the bikes great). We did between 16-40 miles a
  day and no one had a battery issue at all. The assist levels (they were Bosch
  drives) were eco, tour, sport, and turbo, and most of us lived on tour, using
  sport or turbo for occasional inclines).


  I will say, parts of the Portugal ride - you need to be aware - were on
  boardwalks on the beach, some of the boards weren't nailed down well and
  popped as you rode over them giving a sort of piano key effect; in a few cases
  there were exposed nail heads but as long as you rode between the nail heads
  you were fine. Day 1 there were several places where the sand had covered the
  trail/board walk, and we had to slog on walk mode through the sand, not a lot
  of fun. Also there were very extensive cobblestones, which were a real pain,
  but those were mostly limited to days 1 and 2. Nothing you can do about them;
  just to be aware of them and the dunes. I'd say the dunes were maybe a total
  of a couple hundred yards max.

  I hadn't done my homework and didn't realize until the night before that in
  Caminha you had to cross the river on either a ferry (in our case it was not
  running) or a private launch; it was right next to the ferry "terminal" but
  basically you boarded off the beach; super nice guy(s); a max of 6 people and
  maybe 3-4 bikes per trip; took about 10 minutes to cross the river and it was
  an adventure (there WERE life vests!); the heaviest of our boats with about 6
  people and 6 bikes actually ran aground on a sand bar but got off without too
  much consternation. It cost us $7 euro per person with bike.

  We rode to Esposende, Caminha (both through coastal scenery), then to Baiona
  (gorgeous ride and town); Vigo (our extra stop; a city of 290,000) but we
  actually enjoyed the ride in and the city itself. Then to Pontevedra, Caldas
  de Rei, and Santiago de Compostela itself. I thought the entire trip was
  terrific but I enjoyed the last 2-3 days the most. Much of that was through
  forest, small villages, farmland and vineyards. The views through the forest
  of the river after leaving Vigo were terrific. We were, as I said, blown away
  by the service of the Top Bike Tours team and the quality and location of the
  hotels.

  As I said earlier, the only glitches were the GPS got lost en route to the
  Pontevedra Parador; we gave up on it (only happened when we were well into
  town) so we plugged its street address into our Iphone map function and all
  was fine.

  Locals were almost invariably helpful. At least 4 times when we made a wrong
  turn, a local flagged us down and redirected us to the route. As we went
  further along we encountered more and more walkers/hikers, who were invariably
  courteous and shared greetings of "Bon Camino" or "Buen Camino". We literally
  only encountered 2 grouches on the whole trip.
