      ## The six Porto day tours in detail

      ### Porto Downtown and Sightseeing Bike Tour — €36

      Three hours, 7 km / 4.3 mi, from our store above the river down to the Atlantic and back through the city park. Seven kilometres across three hours tells you what kind of ride this is: you are stopping constantly, and the cycling is how you get from one thing to the next. It is the ride most people take, and it is the one to take if this is your first time here. A guide rides with the group the whole way, in English, Portuguese or Spanish, and stops for about twenty minutes partway round. You pass the Palácio da Bolsa, the gothic church of São Francisco, the fortress at the river mouth, and the only city park in Portugal that ends at a beach. **The honest part:** the last stretch climbs back up the city's longest avenue, in traffic, and it is the only thing anyone grumbles about. Minimum age 18.

      ### Porto E-Bike Sightseeing Tour — €52

      The same three-hour, 7 km / 4.3 mi city route on an electric bike. Sixteen euros more buys you the hill at the end for free, which is a reasonable trade if you have been on a plane for nine hours or you simply do not want to arrive back sweating. It is also the fix for a group riding at two different speeds — the e-bikes can run alongside the standard bikes, so nobody has to wait and nobody has to split off. Starts high in the city, so the first few minutes are downhill before you meet the river. Minimum age 18. [More on our electric bikes](/tours/bike-types/e-bike/).

      ### Porto Food and Wine Bike Tour — €108

      Six hours and 7 km / 4.3 mi — built around eating rather than around distance, and the numbers say so plainly. You ride through the centre past São Bento station and its twenty thousand tiles, cross the river into Vila Nova de Gaia for a Port cellar and a tasting, and carry on past Castelo do Queijo to **lunch in the fish quarter at Matosinhos** — grilled sardines, sea bass, bream, whatever came in that morning. Departures at 09:00 or 14:30. The pace is slow and the stops are long, which is the point. **The honest part:** six hours is a long time on a bike if you have not ridden in a while, even at this pace, and the climb back up at the end is the same one the city tours finish on. Minimum age 16.

      ### Mountain Bike Tour in Porto — €103

      We collect you from your accommodation at 08:00 and drive twenty minutes east to the Valongo hills. From there it is schist trails and single track — real off-road riding, not a gravel path — with tracks between 20 and 50 km (12 to 31 miles) set to what your group can actually do. You ride past the schist village of Couce, and if you have the legs for the climb to the top there is a viewpoint that pays for it. **The honest part:** this is the only one of the six that needs off-road skill. Hardtail Trek bikes, van transfer, snacks and hydration are included. Minimum age 16.

      ### Full-Day Bike Tour: Porto to Póvoa de Varzim — €204

      Eight hours, 45 km (28 miles), one direction only — you ride north up the coast from the city and take a transfer home from the far end, which is why it covers ground none of the shorter rides reach. Leaves at 09:00. Past the Port cellars in Gaia, the fortress and Castelo do Queijo, then Siza's tidal pools at Leça da Palmeira, the fish market at Angeiras, the 18th-century church at Labruge, Vila do Conde and the sea inlets at Póvoa. Lunch is grilled fish in Matosinhos and a Port cellar ticket is included. **The honest part:** it is flat, but 45 km is still a full day. Minimum age 18.

      ### Douro Valley Full-Day Ride — €299

      This one leaves Porto behind entirely. We collect you at 08:30 and drive inland to Vila Real, where an old railway line — now a dirt track — runs down through the vineyards to Pinhão. Routes of 25, 50 or 77 km (16, 31 or 48 miles); the first 22 km are downhill, after which you choose between the climb towards Lamego and the flat run to Pinhão. Road bikes and e-bikes are both available, and the e-bike is the usual answer to the climbing. **€299 covers the bike, the van, mechanical support and GPS. A guide riding with your group is €62 extra per group, lunch is €72 per person, and quinta entry tickets are paid locally** — worth knowing before you compare it with the €204 coast day. The day is cycling in the morning, lunch, then viewpoints and one farm. If a single day in the vineyards is not enough, we also ride the valley over a week — see the [multi-day routes in the Douro Valley](/tours/regions/douro-river/).

      ## How long are the tours, and how far?

      **Three hours and 7 km / 4.3 mi is the standard Porto bike tour.** Both city rides — standard bike and e-bike — run exactly three hours over the same 7 km, leave at 10:00 or 14:30, and go at sightseeing pace with a twenty-minute stop in the middle. That is the shape most people are looking for, and it leaves the rest of your day intact.

      **Seven kilometres in three hours is deliberate, and it is worth saying out loud.** That is about an hour and a half of actual pedalling broken up by stops. If you have not been on a bike in years, this is the number that should reassure you — it is a walking tour you happen to do seated. If you were hoping to cover ground, these are the wrong two tours and we would rather tell you now: take the Valongo mountain bike day, the coastal run to Póvoa de Varzim at 45 km / 28 mi, or the Douro Valley at up to 77 km / 48 mi.

      From there it steps up in three jumps: **six hours** for the food and wine ride, at the same gentle pace but with a cellar and a long lunch in it; **eight hours and 45 km (28 miles)** for the one-way coastal run to Póvoa de Varzim; and **a full day out of the city** for the Valongo mountain bike tour, which starts with an 08:00 pickup, or the Douro Valley, which runs 08:30 to roughly 19:00 door to door.

      ## What a day tour in Porto is like

      Most people take the three-hour city ride, so start there. You meet at the store at 10:00 or 14:30. A guide fits you to a bike, hands you a helmet and a water bottle, and then rides with the group the whole way — nobody is handed a map and pointed downhill. Two riders is the minimum for a departure, and guiding is in English, Portuguese or Spanish. There is one stop of about 20 minutes partway around. It is graded easy, and the only real effort comes at the end, climbing back up the city's longest avenue. You are back where you started by early afternoon with the rest of the day still yours.

      The longer days work differently. The food and wine ride runs six hours from a 9:00 or 14:30 start, at a pace built around a Port cellar in Vila Nova de Gaia and a sit-down lunch rather than around distance. The Póvoa de Varzim day is eight hours and 45 km (28 miles), leaving at 9:00 — you ride one direction up the coast and take the transfer back, which is why it covers ground the shorter rides can't. The mountain bike day starts earliest, with a pickup at your accommodation at 8:00 and a 20-minute van transfer out of the city.

      Every guided tour carries liability and personal accident cover. Minimum age is 18 on some rides and 16 on others.

      ## Where you ride

      The two city tours share a shape: down from the store, through Avenida dos Aliados, then west along the Douro until it meets the Atlantic.

      Along the water you pass the Palácio da Bolsa, the gothic bulk of São Francisco and the Ribeira. At the mouth stands the Fortress of São João Baptista, and just past it Praia do Molhe and the small chapel of São Miguel-o-Anjo — a chapel with a lighthouse built into it, which our guides claim as the country's oldest renaissance structure. North along the seafront is Castelo do Queijo, a fort on a round lump of granite. The way back goes inland through Parque da Cidade, which ends at sand rather than at a road, past the garden at the Boavista roundabout, and up to São Martinho de Cedofeita, the city's oldest church.

      The food and wine ride adds São Bento station and crosses into Vila Nova de Gaia for the Port lodges. The Póvoa de Varzim day keeps going north past where the others turn around: Leça da Palmeira and Siza's tidal pools, the fish market at Angeiras, the church at Labruge, Vila do Conde, and the sea inlets at Póvoa.

      Two rides leave the city altogether — schist trails and single track above Valongo, or the old railway line through the vineyards from Vila Real down to Pinhão.

      ## Which Porto tour should I pick?

      **First time in Porto, and you want the city explained.** Take the three-hour Downtown and Sightseeing ride. It covers the riverfront, the river mouth and the coast, it costs €36, and you are free by early afternoon.

      **You are worried about the hills.** Take the same route on an e-bike. Porto is built on a slope and the ride finishes by climbing back up it; €16 more removes that question entirely.

      **You have one day and you want the coast.** The Póvoa de Varzim day is the one that actually gets up the Atlantic shore — 45 km (28 miles) north, with the transfer back so you never have to re-ride it.

      **You came for the food and the Port.** The six-hour Food and Wine ride is slow by design: a cellar, a tasting, a long lunch, and enough riding in between to earn it.

      **You ride properly and you want dirt.** The Valongo mountain bike day is the only off-road ride here. Tracks between 20 and 50 km, adjusted to your group.

      **You want vineyards, not a city.** The Douro Valley day leaves Porto at 08:30 and rides a rail trail down through the terraces. It is the longest day on the list.

      ## Day tours or multi-day from Porto?

      **Short version: if Porto is your base, book a day ride. If Portugal is the trip, book a route.** We run both from this city, and they are not larger and smaller versions of each other. Searching for a Porto bike tour turns up both at once with nothing in between to explain the gap, so here is the gap.

      **A day tour leaves the rest of your plans alone.** You keep your hotel, you keep your evening, you can decide the night before, and it costs between €36 and €299. Take one if you have three nights in the city, if riding is one of the things you want to do rather than the reason you flew, or if you want to know how a day in this terrain feels before you commit a week to it.

      **A route moves you down a coastline.** The multi-day trips that start in Porto run seven to thirteen days and 238 to 493 km (148 to 306 miles): north along the Minho, which is the border with Spain; south down the Silver Coast to Coimbra; or the long Atlantic run to Lisbon. They start at €1,434, your luggage is driven ahead to each night's hotel, and all of them ride guided or self-guided. The daily distance is lower than most people expect — a seven-day route is roughly 35 to 40 km (22 to 25 miles) of riding a day, less than the one-day coastal ride on this page.

      **The test that decides it:** count the nights you are willing to sleep somewhere other than Porto. None, and you want a day tour. Six or more, and you want a route. Two or three, and the day tours are still the better buy — take two of them and keep the room.

      ## Best time of year to cycle in Porto

      **April to June and September to October are the best riding months here**, and September is the one to pick if you can — warm, dry, and the Douro is in harvest.

      **July and August** are busy and hot. The coastal rides are the ones that hold up, because the Atlantic wind does the cooling; a full day inland in August is hard work. Book the 10:00 departure, not the 14:30.

      **November to March** is Porto's wet season, and it rains more here than in the south. The two city tours and the Póvoa de Varzim day run year round regardless, and a damp three-hour ride along the river is a very different proposition from a damp day in the hills. Winter is also when the city is cheapest and emptiest.

      **Any month, book early for a weekend.** Group departures need two riders, so a midweek date in February may need a private booking — which is still only €62 for the city ride.
