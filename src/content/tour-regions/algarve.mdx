      ## Why the Algarve, and when

      Here is the argument, and it's a genuine one rather than a marketing one: **the Algarve is the only part of Portugal you can reliably ride in winter.**

      Porto in January is wet. The Douro is cold and the days are short. Lisbon is mild but grey. The Algarve sits far enough south, and low enough, that December through March is dry, bright and in the high teens Celsius — around 60°F. Not beach weather. Excellent cycling weather.

      For a North American rider this is the whole proposition. You are not choosing between the Algarve in February and the Douro in February. You are choosing between the Algarve in February and not riding.

      It also means you avoid the Algarve's other season. July and August on this coast are hot, crowded and expensive. Ride it in October or February and you get the same coastline with the volume turned down.

      ## Coast or interior?

      The honest answer is that we ride the coast, and here's why.

      **The Vicentine coast** is protected parkland — the Southwest Alentejo and Vicentine Coast Natural Park. That designation is the reason it looks the way it does: no resorts, no ribbon development, a road network that stayed small. Cliffs, storks nesting on sea stacks, fishing villages that are still fishing villages. It's the best riding in southern Portugal and it isn't close.

      **The interior** — the Serra de Monchique and the barrocal behind the coast — is quieter still, and genuinely beautiful, with cork oak and orange groves and hill villages. It also climbs. Monchique rises to around 900 m and the roads up it are a serious morning's work. It's a road cyclist's region rather than a touring region, and it's not where our route goes.

      If you want the interior specifically, say so when you enquire and we'll tell you honestly whether we can build it.

      ## What the riding is like

      **The wind is the defining feature and nobody else will tell you this.** The Atlantic coast of Portugal has a prevailing northerly — the *nortada* — that blows most reliably from spring through summer. Our route runs north to south, which means it is behind you. That's not luck; it's why the route is built in that direction. On a good day it will push you along for an hour without you noticing.

      On a bad day it's a crosswind off the sea on an exposed cliff road and it's tiring in a way that distance isn't. Budget for one of those days in a week. It is the single thing most likely to make a stage harder than the number suggests.

      **Surfaces.** A mix of quiet paved road and unsurfaced coastal track — sand, packed dirt, some rough sections. Not technical, but not a road-bike route. Our guests consistently mention the dirt roads as a highlight rather than a complaint. Wide tyres, which is what we fit.

      **Terrain.** Rolling rather than flat, with short sharp pitches where the road drops to a river mouth and climbs back out. No sustained climbs. Graded 2/5 for a reason.

      **Traffic.** Light to nonexistent for most of the week. It picks up around Lagos on the last day.

      **How we grade difficulty.** *Easy* is flat, under 50 km a day. *Easy to moderate* is 50–75 km a day with short climbs on up to 30% of the route. *Moderate* adds dirt or trail and climbing on up to 40%. [Full scale →](/faqs/)

      **E-bikes** are available and take the wind out of the equation entirely, which on this coast is worth more than it is anywhere else in Portugal. [E-bike options →](/tours/bike-types/e-bike/)

      ## Best time of year

      | Months | Riding conditions |
      |---|---|
      | **Oct–Nov** | Probably the best window. Warm sea, mild air, coast empty, light winds. |
      | **Dec–Feb** | The Algarve's real advantage. Dry, bright, mid-to-high teens Celsius / around 60°F. Short days, so plan shorter stages. Nowhere else in Portugal offers this. |
      | **Mar–May** | Wildflowers on the cliffs, green interior, rising temperatures. Excellent, and the nortada starts building. |
      | **Jun–Aug** | Hot, busy, expensive. Rideable if you start early. We'd steer you to another month. |
      | **Sep** | Still warm, crowds gone, water at its warmest. |
