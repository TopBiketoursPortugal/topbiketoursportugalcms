      ## How hard is the Douro? The honest answer, and it is not one answer

      Every operator on this page dodges this. Here is the whole of it.

      **The valley is steep, and three of our four trips are properly hard.** The riverside road is the flat part and you do not stay on it. The quintas are up on the terraces, the villages are on the ridges, and every time a route leaves the water it climbs — not for 500 m, but for several kilometres at a time.

      **One week in the Douro is genuinely easy, and now we can prove it rather than assert it.** The [Douro Valley Wine Cycling Tour](/tours/bike-tours-douro-valley-easy/) is graded **Easy (1/5)**, and the published climbing figures are the reason: **1,895 m / 6,217 ft across the whole week**, against **7,500 m / 24,606 ft** on the 8-day route. That is a quarter of the climbing, and about **270 m a day** spread over 133 km / 83 mi in seven days — roughly 19 km / 12 mi on a riding day, with the longest stage, Penedono to Marialva, at 27 km / 17 mi.

      **Being straight about what "easy" means here.** The ground is still Douro ground — the tour page calls it *"mostly steep, up and down"* and it is. What makes the week easy is not flat terrain, it is short days: you climb for a while, and then you have arrived. The climbing is real, it is just rationed. You are in the same valley, looking at the same terraces, drinking the same port — you are not riding 70 km to get to it.

      **The gentle day option is a rail trail.** The [full-day ride](/tours/douro-valley-full-day-bike-ride/) starts in Vila Real and follows a disused railway line, now a dirt track, down the valley. Rail grades are gentle by definition — trains cannot climb. It is graded Moderate, it is from €299, and it is the right call if you are based in Porto or on a river cruise and want one good day on a bike rather than a week.

      **And then the hard ones, which we are not going to soften.** The [Douro Valley Bike Tour](/tours/bike-tours-douro-valley/) is **Moderate to hard (4/5)**: eight days, 270 km / 168 mi, **7,500 m / 24,606 ft of climbing** — about 1,070 m a day — and a 74 km / 46 mi day through hill country from Pinhão to Vila Nova de Foz Côa that arrives early in the week and decides how you feel about the rest of it. [Trás-os-Montes & Douro International](/tours/bike-tours-portugal-douro-wine/) is **Hard (5/5)**: 369 km / 229 mi in seven days with **6,392 m / 20,971 ft** of climbing, and the hardest tour on our books. Both are excellent. Neither is a first bike holiday.

      **E-bikes move the hard ones within reach.** This is the region where they matter most: on an e-bike a 4/5 Douro route rides like a 2/5, and the climbs stop being events. If you want the big route and you are honest with yourself about the gradient, take the e-bike. Nobody in the group will care. [E-bike options →](/tours/bike-types/e-bike/)

      **Our grading scale, exactly as it is published on every tour page:** *Easy (1/5)* — mostly flat, daily distances up to 50 km / 31 mi, for inexperienced cyclists. *Easy to moderate (2/5)* — mostly flat, 50–75 km / 31–47 mi, with short climbs on up to 30% of the route. *Moderate (3/5)* — may include dirt roads or trails, 50–75 km, with climbs on up to 40%. *Moderate to hard (4/5)* — 50–75 km, moderate climbs on up to 60%, for cyclists who have done some fitness preparation. *Hard (5/5)* — 50–100 km / 31–62 mi, strong climbs on up to 60%, for experienced cyclists.       ## Wine on a Douro bike tour

      The quintas are the point. The routes are built to pass them, and the multi-day wine tours include visits and tastings along the way — port is fortified, so the tastings are small, and the estates are used to cyclists arriving in the middle of the day.

      Two practical notes nobody else writes down. First, the quintas are uphill: the tasting comes *after* the climb, not before. Second, port is around 20% alcohol, and the descent back to the river is not the place to discover that. Our guides pace this. On self-guided days, pace it yourself.

      *Whether tastings are in the tour price or paid on the day varies by route — [ask us when you enquire](/contacts/).*

      ## Best time of year to cycle the Douro: month by month

      | Months | Riding conditions |
      |---|---|
      | **Apr–Jun** | Green terraces, mild temperatures, water still high. Excellent. |
      | **Jul–Aug** | The Douro is a heat trap — the valley walls hold it in and it regularly passes 35°C / 95°F. Ride at dawn or do not ride. |
      | **Sep–Oct** | **The season.** Harvest — *vindima* — is underway, the quintas are working, the terraces turn gold. This is when to come, and it books out first. |
      | **Nov–Mar** | Cold, wet, short days. The valley is beautiful in winter and it is not a cycling proposition. |
