      ## The three tours in detail

      ### Vicentine Coast and Algarve · 8 days · 378 km / 235 mi · Easy to moderate · from €1,746

      **The only tour where Lisbon is the start line.** You arrive and sleep in the city, then ride out the next morning — Lisboa to Troia, 65 km / 40 mi — and keep going south down the Alentejo coast to the Vicentine cliffs and into the Algarve, finishing in Lagos.

      It suits riders who want Lisbon as a bookend rather than the whole trip, and who like the idea of leaving a capital city behind under their own power on the first morning. Graded Easy to moderate: the coast is rolling rather than steep, and the surfaces are good. **The honest line:** that first day out of the city is 65 km / 40 mi, a kilometre short of the longest day of the week, and it comes when your legs are still on aeroplane time. Guided €2,491.

      ### Portugal Atlantic Coast · 8 days · 394 km / 245 mi · Moderate · from €1,743

      Eight days down the Atlantic from São Pedro de Moel to Cascais, on a route the tour page describes as roughly 30% bike lane, 30% quiet paved road and 10% coastal dirt — mostly flat with short climbs. The last two riding days are the good ones for anyone coming for Lisbon: **Ericeira to Sintra, 27 km / 17 mi**, then **Sintra to Cascais, 40 km / 25 mi**.

      Graded Moderate, which is a step up from the two tours either side of it and is mostly about the dirt sections and the exposure on the coast. It is the shorter way to arrive into the Lisbon area by bike if two weeks is more than you have. **The honest line:** the Atlantic wind is a factor on this coast and it is usually behind you going south — which is also why we do not run it the other way. Guided €2,472.

      ### Porto to Lisbon · 13 days · 493 km / 306 mi · Easy to moderate · from €1,644

      The full north-to-south traverse, and the easiest long tour we run. Eleven riding days averaging 45 km / 28 mi: Porto to Furadouro (47 km / 29 mi), Aveiro (45 km / 28 mi), Praia de Mira (40 km / 25 mi), Figueira da Foz (50 km / 31 mi), São Pedro de Moel (65 km / 40 mi), Nazaré (29 km / 18 mi), Óbidos (47 km / 29 mi), Peniche (35 km / 22 mi), Ericeira (68 km / 42 mi), Sintra (27 km / 17 mi) and Cascais (40 km / 25 mi). **Those eleven stages add up to exactly the headline 493 km**, which is worth saying because not every tour on this site does.

      Graded Easy to moderate — flat for days at a time, short climbs, nothing over 68 km. It suits riders who have two weeks and want distance without turning the holiday into a fitness project. Eighteen reviews, 5.0 average. **If 13 days is too many**, there is a reduced version: 308 km over 8 days, finishing in Óbidos. Guided €2,372.

      ## How hard is a Lisbon bike tour? Hills, surfaces and grades, stated honestly

      **Lisbon the city is hilly. The riding we do around it mostly is not.** That sounds like a dodge, so here is the detail: the seven hills are in the old centre, and none of our routes send you up them. What you actually ride is the Tagus riverfront, the Atlantic coast, and the Sintra hills — and only the last of those climbs seriously.

      **The one real climb is Sintra**, which appears on two of the three tours. The stage into it is short — 27 km / 17 mi on the Atlantic Coast tour — and that is deliberate, because it is the hilliest thing on either route. It is a hill, it is unavoidable if you want Sintra, and Sintra is worth it.

      **Traffic and surfaces.** The coast between Cascais and Lisbon has a continuous separated path and is the best-protected riding on the route. North of Ericeira it is quiet paved road. The Atlantic Coast tour includes some coastal dirt — firm, not technical, but it is not tarmac and you should know that before you pick your tyres.

      **Do you need an e-bike?** For the Vicentine Coast and Porto to Lisbon, both graded 2/5, no — these are not hard weeks. For the Atlantic Coast at 3/5, or for Sintra on a hot day, an e-bike is the difference between enjoying the climb and enduring it. [E-bike options →](/tours/bike-types/e-bike/)

      **Our grading scale, exactly as it is published on every tour page:** *Easy (1/5)* — mostly flat, daily distances up to 50 km / 31 mi, for inexperienced cyclists. *Easy to moderate (2/5)* — mostly flat, 50–75 km / 31–47 mi, with short climbs on up to 30% of the route. *Moderate (3/5)* — may include dirt roads or trails, 50–75 km, with climbs on up to 40%. *Moderate to hard (4/5)* — 50–75 km, moderate climbs on up to 60%, for cyclists who have done some fitness preparation. *Hard (5/5)* — 50–100 km / 31–62 mi, strong climbs on up to 60%, for experienced cyclists.       ## Sintra and Cascais by bike

      Both are ride days on our tours rather than separate trips, and they are the reason people remember the Lisbon end of the route.

      **Sintra** is the climb and the payoff: a wooded hill town of palaces above the coastal plain, reached on a short stage precisely because of the gradient. **Cascais** is where the riding stops on two of the three tours — a seafront town at the mouth of the Tagus, with the separated coast path running east from it back towards the city. Riding that path is the gentlest cycling anywhere near Lisbon, and it is how both arrival tours spend their last hours on a bike.

      If you want either one, pick [Portugal Atlantic Coast](/tours/atlantic-coast-to-lisbon-bike-tour/) or [Porto to Lisbon](/tours/porto-to-lisbon-bike-tour/) — both ride Sintra and finish in Cascais.

      ## Best time of year for a Lisbon bike tour: month by month

      | Months | Riding conditions |
      |---|---|
      | **Mar–May** | The best of the year. Mild, green, long light, and the coast is quiet. |
      | **Jun** | Warm and reliable, still comfortable on the bike. Busy in Lisbon itself. |
      | **Jul–Aug** | Hot — regularly above 32°C / 90°F inland, cooler on the Atlantic. The coast is rideable; the Sintra climb in August is not fun. Ride early. |
      | **Sep–Oct** | **The other best season.** Warm sea, thinner crowds, stable weather. Books out early. |
      | **Nov–Feb** | Mild by northern European standards and genuinely wet. Some riders love it. Most should not plan a first bike holiday here now. |

      ## Guided or self-guided from Lisbon? What each one includes

      **Self-guided** is the default and how most people ride these routes. You get the bike, the hotels, your bags moved between them, a GPS unit with the route loaded and the Ride With GPS app, airport transfers, and a phone number. You leave when you like and stop where you like.

      **Guided** adds a guide riding with the group, snacks, group meals and insurance. **The guided minimum is 4 riders. The support van is a separate threshold at 8 riders** — below that there is a guide and no van, which matters if someone in your group might want a lift mid-afternoon. These are two different numbers and they are worth asking about if you are a mixed-ability couple.

      Self-guided does not include insurance. We say so plainly because some operators do not. [More on self-guided bike tours →](/tours/tags/self-guided/)
