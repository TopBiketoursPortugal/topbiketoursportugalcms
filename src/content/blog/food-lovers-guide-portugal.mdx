Here’s your guide to the must-try Portuguese dishes and where to find them.

## **Iconic Portuguese Dishes to Try**

Portuguese cuisine is built on tradition, with recipes passed down through generations. While each region has its unique flavors, some dishes are loved across the country.

### **Bacalhau — Portugal’s Signature Dish**

Bacalhau, or salted cod, is a Portuguese staple with over 365 ways to prepare it — one for each day of the year. Popular versions include:

* **Bacalhau à Brás** — Shredded cod cooked with onions, potatoes, and eggs.
* **Bacalhau com Natas** — A creamy, comforting dish made with cod, cream, and potatoes.
* **Bacalhau à Lagareiro** — Baked cod drizzled with olive oil and roasted garlic, often found in Lisbon’s seafood restaurants.

For an authentic taste, visit Cervejaria Ramiro in Lisbon or Adega São Nicolau in Porto, both known for their expertly prepared cod dishes.

### **Francesinha — Porto’s Famous Sandwich**

A must-try in Porto, the Francesinha is a rich, meat-filled sandwich covered in melted cheese and smothered in a spicy beer sauce. It’s indulgent, messy, and unforgettable. Café Santiago and Bufete Fase are the top spots for this iconic dish.

### **Cataplana — Algarve’s Flavorful Seafood Stew**

For seafood lovers, **Cataplana** is a must. This Algarve specialty combines clams, shrimp, and fish with a rich tomato and garlic broth. Many restaurants in coastal towns like Faro and Tavira are known for their delicious cataplana dishes.

## **Portuguese Snacks and Pastries**

Portugal’s snack culture is just as impressive as its main dishes. Whether you’re exploring city streets or relaxing at a seaside café, these quick bites are perfect for on-the-go indulgence.

### **Pastel de Nata — Portugal’s Famous Custard Tart**

No visit to Portugal is complete without trying a Pastel de Nata. These flaky pastries are filled with rich, creamy custard and topped with a caramelized crust. For the best experience:

* **Pastéis de Belém** in Lisbon is the most famous spot for this iconic pastry.
* **Manteigaria** offers equally delicious tarts, often served warm for the ultimate indulgence.

<SnippetsAstroImage image_path="/src/assets/images/pastel-de-nata.jpg" image_alt="Pastel de Nata"/>

### **Conservas — Portugal’s Gourmet Tinned Seafood**

Though often overlooked, Conservas are a true Portuguese delicacy. Premium canned sardines, mackerel, and octopus are beautifully preserved in olive oil and spices, making them a flavorful snack or light meal. Boutique shops like A Conserveira de Lisboa in Lisbon are perfect for discovering these gourmet treats.

### **Bolos de São Gonçalo — A Northern Delight**

In Amarante, the quirky Bolos de São Gonçalo is a must-try pastry. Known for its unique design and rich filling, it’s a local favorite during festive celebrations.

## **Portuguese Seafood: A Coastal Delight**

With its long Atlantic coastline, Portugal is famous for fresh seafood dishes that reflect the country’s coastal lifestyle.

### **Sardinhas Assadas — Grilled Sardines**

Sardinhas Assadas are a staple during Lisbon’s summer festivals, where sardines are grilled over open flames and served with crusty bread. This simple yet flavorful dish is a must-try during Portugal’s popular Festa de Santo António.

<SnippetsAstroImage image_path="/src/assets/images/sardinhas-assadas.jpg" image_alt="Sardinhas Assadas"/>

### **Polvo à Lagareiro — A Tender Octopus Delight**

For something special, try Polvo à Lagareiro, a tender octopus dish baked with olive oil, garlic, and roasted potatoes. It’s commonly served in coastal towns like Matosinhos and Lagos.

### **Arroz de Marisco — Rich Seafood Rice**

Similar to paella but with a Portuguese twist, Arroz de Marisco combines rice with shrimp, mussels, and clams in a rich tomato sauce. For the best experience, visit Solar dos Presuntos in Lisbon.

## **Dining Like a Local in Portugal**

To experience authentic Portuguese cuisine, dining like a local is key. Visiting traditional markets, local taverns, and regional food festivals offers a chance to savor fresh, seasonal ingredients.

### **Local Markets to Explore**

* **Mercado da Ribeira** in Lisbon offers a mix of traditional vendors and gourmet food stalls.
* **Bolhão Market** in Porto is perfect for sampling fresh seafood, cheeses, and pastries.

## **Portuguese Wines to Pair with Your Meals**

Portuguese cuisine is best enjoyed with a glass of local wine. Popular options include:

* **Vinho Verde** — A light, slightly sparkling wine that pairs well with grilled sardines.
* **Port Wine** — A rich, fortified wine from Porto that’s perfect with desserts like **Pastel de Nata**.
* **Douro Valley Reds** — Full-bodied wines ideal for hearty meat dishes.
